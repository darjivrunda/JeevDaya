Animal First Aid Reference Guide

What you must and must NOT do while waiting for medical help.

Hit & Run / Severe Traumatic Injury

1. Do not move the animal abruptly: Spine or pelvis fractures can worsen if carried incorrectly. Gently slide a thick blanket, wooden plank, or jacket underneath them to serve as a makeshift stretcher.
2. Control active bleeding: Apply firm, steady pressure directly over the bleeding wound using a clean towel or cloth. Do not apply tight tourniquets unless instructed by a vet.
3. Prevent shock: Keep the animal warm and in a quiet, low-stress area away from crowds and traffic.

Severe Heatstroke & Dehydration (Summer)

1. Move to shade immediately: Relocate the dog or animal to a well-ventilated, shaded spot.
2. Cool gradually: Pour normal room-temperature water over their paws, groin, and neck. Never use freezing ice water, as it causes blood vessels to constrict and traps heat internally.
3. Offer small sips: Offer clean water or ORS (oral rehydration salts). If unconscious, do not force liquid into their mouth.

Injured Birds (Glass Collisions or Kite String / Manja)

1. Gently catch: Throw a light cloth or towel over the bird to safely restrain wings.
2. Do not pull embedded string: If kite string (manja) is wrapped around wings or neck, cut excess string with scissors, but do not forcefully yank threads embedded in deep flesh.
3. Cardboard sanctuary: Place the bird in a ventilated cardboard box with small air holes and a soft towel at the bottom. Keep in a dark, quiet room. Do not force water down their beak.

Suspected Poisoning or Chemical Ingestion

1. Identify the substance: If possible, safely note or take a photo of the packaging/poison (rat bait, insecticide, chemical spill).
2. Do NOT induce vomiting: Caustic chemicals and petroleum distillates cause severe burns on the esophagus if vomited back up.
3. Immediate Vet Transport: Poison cases require intravenous antidotes, activated charcoal, and fluids within the first 60 minutes.
